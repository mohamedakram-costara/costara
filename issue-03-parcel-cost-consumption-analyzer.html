Parcel Cost Is High. Stop Calling More Suppliers.

Lowest unit price is not always the lowest delivered-meal cost.

Do not blame price before measuring usage

When parcel cost rises, the immediate reaction is often to request more quotations. That attacks only one variable: purchase price. Total parcel cost is also driven by how many pieces are issued, damaged, double-packed, mismatched, substituted or used for meals that did not require them.

Separate price variance from usage variance

Compare budget quantity and price with actual quantity and price. If unit price increased, quantify that effect separately. Then quantify excess consumption using actual pieces versus justified meal requirements. This prevents market inflation or geopolitical supply pressure from being used as an explanation for internal operational leakage.

Cheap packaging can become expensive

A lower-priced container may leak, crack, require double packing, create complaints or force meal replacement. Procurement should therefore compare successful-pack cost, not only unit price. Specification, quality failure rate, pack quantity, lid compatibility and operational handling all influence the true cost.

More suppliers are not always diversification

Ten suppliers may still depend on the same manufacturer, importer, material or country of origin. Resilience can require supplier diversification, manufacturer alternatives, material substitution, specification redesign and better demand planning. The objective is supply continuity at the lowest sustainable total cost.

Costara approach

Track containers and lids issued against meals requiring packaging, record damage and repacking, calculate price and usage variance separately, and review specification performance. Negotiate price after the operation proves its consumption discipline.

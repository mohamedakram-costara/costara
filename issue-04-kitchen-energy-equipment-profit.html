Kitchen Energy & Equipment Efficiency

Finance does not turn on your oven — but your oven can turn off your profit.

Budgeting is an operational responsibility too

Finance reports utility spend, but operations decide when equipment is switched on, how fully it is loaded, how many batches are required and whether capacity matches production demand. LPG and electricity therefore belong in the operating conversation, not only the finance report.

Use energy cost per meal

A kitchen with a lower monthly electricity bill is not automatically more efficient if it produces far fewer meals. Divide energy cost by meals produced and, where useful, compare cost per 1,000 meals. This normalizes volume and makes site-to-site or month-to-month comparison more meaningful.

Right-size equipment to demand

Oversized equipment may carry higher capital, maintenance, ventilation and energy cost while operating at low productive utilisation. Undersized equipment may require extra batches, longer hours, overtime and duplicate equipment. The best decision is the capacity that supports required volume and service at the lowest total cost.

Idle time is a cost driver

Preheating too early, running half-empty ovens, unnecessary hot holding, inefficient exhaust schedules and poor production sequencing can create utility cost without equivalent output. Production planning and menu scheduling can therefore become energy-management tools.

Costara approach

Record equipment capacity, actual load, operating hours, idle hours, batches, energy use and meals produced. Compare productive utilisation with cost per meal, then test whether scheduling, load consolidation or equipment right-sizing can reduce total cost without harming quality or safety.

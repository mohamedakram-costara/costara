Waste-to-Value: Where Profit Becomes Garbage

Do not start with the bin. Trace where the value was lost.

Waste is a financial event

Waste reporting often starts when food reaches the bin. By then the business has already paid for purchasing, receiving, storage, preparation, cooking, labour, utilities and sometimes packaging. The more useful question is not only “how many kilograms were wasted?” but “where in the process did paid-for value become unrecoverable?”

Separate the causes

Classify waste by stage: receiving rejection, storage expiry, trimming and yield loss, preparation error, overproduction, portioning, unserved returns, quality failure and plate waste. Different causes require different owners and different corrective actions. One overall waste percentage hides the operational source.

Use waste cost per meal

Waste kilograms matter operationally; waste value matters commercially. Convert the period waste value into cost per meal and track it by category. A change of only a few fils per meal can become a meaningful monthly amount when multiplied across high-volume catering operations.

Recover value before disposal

The highest-value opportunity is prevention: better forecasting, yield control, production planning, portion discipline and shelf-life management. Where company policy, food-safety rules and local regulation permit, approved surplus-management, recycling or waste-contractor arrangements may create secondary value. Unsafe food must never be reintroduced simply to save cost.

Costara approach

Trace the waste back to its process origin, attach AED value, identify the accountable function, estimate the realistically preventable portion and track action. The objective is not “zero waste” at any cost; it is controlled production, safe food and lower avoidable loss.

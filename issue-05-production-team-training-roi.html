Costara Insights • Issue 05 • People

Train the Team. Protect the Cost.

The person holding the knife can influence food cost before the report is generated.

The production line creates cost before Finance reports it

Cost controllers can calculate yield and variance, but production employees physically determine trimming, portion size, batch accuracy and product handling. A few percentage points of yield loss across high-volume meat, poultry, fish or vegetables can materially influence monthly food cost.

Experience and certification are different assets

A certificate alone does not create efficiency, and years of experience alone do not guarantee standardisation. The stronger model is to identify practical experience, assess skill gaps, provide targeted training, apply the learning, measure the result and support recognized certification where appropriate.

Training needs a financial outcome

Before and after measures can include yield percentage, waste kilograms, portion variance, production error, rework, output per labour hour and food-safety nonconformities. This allows training cost to be compared with the operational value created rather than treated only as an HR activity.

Retention also protects cost

When experienced employees leave, the business can absorb recruitment, onboarding, lower initial productivity, additional supervision, mistakes and loss of site knowledge. Development and progression can therefore protect both production efficiency and replacement cost.

Costara approach

Train to a measured operational gap. Link skills development to yield, waste, portioning, equipment use, food safety and basic cost awareness. Recognize employees who convert learning into sustained performance.

Costara Insights • Issue 09 • Food Safety

HACCP & CCP: Margin Protection System

Control the critical. Protect the product. Protect the profit.

HACCP is not only a compliance procedure

HACCP protects consumers by controlling significant food-safety hazards. Strong execution can also protect the financial value already invested in raw materials, labour, utilities, production time and distribution. Food safety and cost control should therefore support each other, never compete.

Control failures can become financial losses

Temperature abuse, cross-contamination, inadequate cooking, poor cooling, traceability gaps or receiving failures can lead to rejection, batch disposal, rework, replacement meals, complaints and operational disruption. Each event can carry a direct cost in addition to the food-safety risk.

CCP records create useful operational evidence

Monitoring records can reveal recurring equipment problems, process delays, temperature instability, training gaps or handling failures. Cost control can use this evidence to understand repeated loss without interfering with food-safety authority or compromising standards.

Traceability can limit the scale of loss

When batches, ingredients and production records are clear, an investigation can isolate affected product more accurately. Weak traceability can make the financial impact of a food-safety event larger because the business may be unable to distinguish safe product from potentially affected product.

Costara approach

Protect the consumer first. Then measure the operational consequence of failures: waste, rework, replacement, lost production time, additional delivery and disruption. HACCP and CCP are not only about compliance — disciplined control also protects product value, continuity and margin.

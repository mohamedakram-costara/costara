Catering Food Cost Control

Food cost is not only a monthly percentage. It is the financial result of purchasing, yield, production, portioning, waste and service decisions.

Start with the real cost driver

Separate purchase-price variance from usage, yield, waste, portion, substitution, production and service losses. A lower supplier price cannot fix excess consumption inside the operation.

Measure cost per meal

Total food cost becomes more useful when connected to meals served, menu category, production volume and contract revenue. Compare theoretical recipe cost with actual consumption and explain the gap.

Control before month-end

Daily balance checks, production planning, yield control and issue-to-meal reconciliation allow action before the monthly food-cost report arrives.

Costara principle

Measure the process, identify the controllable loss, calculate the financial impact, and convert it into an action plan.

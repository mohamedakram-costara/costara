The Hidden Cost of Catering Distribution

Your food cost may be under control — but is your delivery model eating your profit?

Why delivery belongs in cost control

A meal is not financially complete when it leaves the kitchen. In large-scale catering, distribution can add vehicle hire, fuel, driver and helper time, loading labour, waiting time, security access delays, temperature-holding requirements, damaged packaging and failed or repeated deliveries. If management reviews only food cost, these expenses can sit outside the conversation even though they influence the real margin of every meal.

Measure the cost of the delivered meal

Start with total distribution cost for the period and divide it by the number of meals that genuinely required delivery. Then separate route cost, vehicle cost, labour, fuel, overtime, waiting time, re-delivery and rejected-meal cost. This creates a more useful KPI than total transport spend because it can be compared across sites, routes and months.

Do not optimize routes blindly

Fewer routes are not automatically better. Consolidation must still protect food-safety limits, service deadlines, security access requirements, vehicle capacity and product quality. The right question is: what route structure delivers the required meal safely and on time at the lowest total cost?

Management questions

Which routes repeatedly operate below useful capacity? Which locations create the most waiting time? How many kilometres are travelled empty? What is the cost of one additional trip? Are delivery windows aligned with production completion? Are vehicle types matched to actual load? These questions convert logistics from a support activity into a measurable profit lever.

Costara approach

Map demand by location, record actual trip cost, measure load utilisation and waiting time, then calculate cost per delivered meal. Test improvement scenarios without compromising food safety or service. A saving is credible only when it survives both operational and financial review.
